EU AI Act: what changed on 2 August 2026,
and what it means for your testing.

The Article 50 transparency obligations and active GPAI enforcement powers are now in force. Adversarial testing is not a conformity assessment, and nobody should sell you one dressed up as a pentest. What it does is show whether the controls you have declared actually hold when somebody attacks them.

Vantixia provides security testing evidence. We do not provide legal advice, and we do not perform conformity assessment. Nothing on this page is a legal opinion on your obligations under the EU AI Act. Whether the Act applies to you, and what it requires of you, is a question for your legal counsel. 01

//: 01 WHAT TOOK EFFECT

2 August 2026 moved the Act from text to enforcement

Two things changed on that date. The Article 50 transparency obligations came into force, and the enforcement powers over general-purpose AI became active. Until then, most teams treated the Act as a roadmap item. It now has teeth behind it.

Penalties reach EUR 15 million or 3% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher. For most companies shipping an AI feature, the turnover figure is the one that matters, and it is the reason procurement teams have started asking for AI test evidence that did not appear in a security questionnaire eighteen months ago.

The practical effect is that the transparency behaviours you designed into the system are no longer only a product decision. They are a control, and a control that has never been attacked is a control nobody has evidence for.

A short self-assessment before you scope anything

The Act follows the market, not the mailing address. If your system reaches people in the EU, the obligations travel with it regardless of where you are headquartered. A team in Bangalore, Austin or Singapore is in the same position as a team in Dublin if EU users can reach the product.

03

//: 03 WHAT TESTING EVIDENCES

Not a conformity assessment. Evidence that your controls hold under attack.

This distinction matters, so it is worth being blunt about it. Adversarial testing is not itself a conformity assessment. It is how you evidence that your disclosure, marking and guardrail controls hold under attack rather than only in the happy path. Those are two different claims, and only one of them is ours to make.

DISCLOSURE

Does the disclosure survive contact?

Your assistant tells users it is an AI when asked plainly. We ask it in the ways an attacker would: through injected instructions, roleplay framing, translation wrappers and hidden content in retrieved documents. Then we record what it said.

MARKING

Can the marking be stripped?

If your pipeline marks synthetic content, the control is only as good as its weakest path. We look for output routes that skip the marking step, inputs that suppress it, and formats where it silently does not survive the round trip.

GUARDRAILS

Do the guardrails hold, or only appear to?

Most guardrails pass the demo. We test the chains: injection through a retrieved document, a tool call that widens what the model can reach, a jailbreak that moves the system outside the purpose you declared for it.

//: 04 SCOPE AND DELIVERABLES

What we test, and what lands on your desk

What we test

  • Direct prompt injection against disclosure and refusal behaviour
  • Indirect injection through documents your retrieval pipeline ingests
  • Attempts to suppress, strip or bypass synthetic-content marking
  • System prompt and hidden-context extraction
  • Jailbreak chains that move the system outside its declared purpose
  • Tool and agent abuse, where the model can act rather than only answer
  • Output handling downstream of the model, where the real damage usually lands

What you receive

  • Every finding mapped to the control it affects, with reproduction steps
  • Severity and business impact stated per finding, not per category
  • Remediation guidance written for the engineers who own the fix
  • A free retest after you remediate, to confirm the findings are closed
  • An attestation letter: a signed letter of scope, dates, methodology and remediation status. It attests to what we tested, not that the system is secure
  • A walkthrough call so your legal and product people hear it in plain language

//: 06 QUESTIONS

What teams ask us about the Act

Does a penetration test satisfy the EU AI Act for us?+

No, and be careful with anyone who tells you otherwise. Vantixia provides security testing evidence. We do not provide legal advice and we do not perform conformity assessment. Adversarial testing is not itself a conformity assessment. It is how you evidence that your disclosure, marking and guardrail controls hold under attack rather than only in the happy path. Your legal counsel decides what the Act requires of you. We produce the technical evidence they and your auditors ask for.

We are not based in the EU. Does any of this reach us?+

Possibly, and that is a question for your counsel rather than for us. What we can tell you is the pattern we see in scoping calls: teams that operate a chatbot reachable by EU users, generate synthetic content for EU audiences, or deploy a general-purpose model into the EU market are being asked for evidence regardless of where they are headquartered. Reach into the market is what drives the questions, not the address on the incorporation documents.

What can testing actually evidence for Article 50 transparency?+

Three things, concretely. First, whether your disclosure behaviour survives an adversarial attempt to remove it, rather than only answering correctly when a cooperative user asks. Second, whether synthetic-content marking can be stripped, suppressed or routed around. Third, whether the guardrails behind both of those hold when the input is hostile. Each becomes a finding with reproduction steps, or a documented attempt that did not succeed.

How does this sit alongside our legal counsel and our conformity work?+

Cleanly, because the roles do not overlap. Counsel determines which obligations apply and what your declared controls need to be. Your conformity route is decided and run separately. We come in underneath both and answer a narrower technical question: given the controls you have declared, do they hold when someone attacks them, and where exactly do they fail. Most teams hand our report straight to whoever is assembling their evidence file.

What are the penalties if we get this wrong?+

Penalties reach EUR 15 million or 3% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher. We are quoting the figure, not interpreting how it would be applied to your situation, which is again a question for counsel. In practice the commercial pressure arrives earlier than any regulator does: enterprise procurement teams have started asking for AI test evidence as a condition of the contract.

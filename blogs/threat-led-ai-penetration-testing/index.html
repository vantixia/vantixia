Threat-Led AI Penetration Testing: From Scope to Report

A real AI pentest is scoped, threat-led and evidence-driven - not a weekend of jailbreak screenshots.

Testing AI By RAJNISH KUMAR • 26 Apr 2026 • 8 min read

A methodology tells you how to attack a model. An engagement tells you how to run a professional test end to end - from the first scoping call to the retest that confirms fixes held. When the deliverable is a report a client will act on and pay for, the process around the testing matters as much as the testing itself.

Threat-led means the engagement is organised around plausible adversaries and the outcomes they want, not around a checklist of prompts. That framing keeps effort pointed at the risks that actually matter to this system.

Scoping an AI system

AI scope is broader than "the model." It includes the system prompt and its handling, the retrieval sources and how they are populated, the tools and functions the model can invoke, the identity and permissions those actions run under, and the surrounding application. Miss the tool-calling layer and you have scoped out the part most likely to turn a prompt into real-world impact.

Nail down what is in bounds, what data is real versus synthetic, whether you are testing against production or a staging mirror, and who owns the downstream systems the model can reach. Those answers shape everything that follows.

Rules of engagement

AI testing has failure modes that traditional rules of engagement do not cover, so spell them out. Because the model can trigger tools, an attack can cause side effects - data writes, external calls, spend. Agree in advance what actions are permitted, what must be simulated, and where the hard stops are. Establish handling for any sensitive data the model exposes, a deconfliction contact, and a testing window. Get written authorisation naming the specific systems. Without that paperwork you are not testing, you are trespassing.

Threat-led planning

Before touching the target, build the threat picture. Who would attack this system, and to what end - exfiltrating other users' data, abusing the tools for fraud, degrading the service, extracting the proprietary prompt? Map each adversary goal to concrete attack paths through the components you scoped. That map becomes your test plan, and it tells the client why each test exists in terms they recognise.

Execution with evidence

Testing without evidence is an opinion. Every finding needs a reproducible artefact that a developer can follow to the same result.

  • Exact inputs: the prompt, document, or media that triggered the behaviour, captured verbatim.
  • Observed output: the model's response or the action it took, recorded in full.
  • Reproduction steps: the path from application entry point to impact, so it can be replayed.
  • Reliability: how consistently the attack succeeds, since a one-in-twenty break reads very differently from a reliable one.

Attribute each finding to the component that failed. "The model said something bad" is weak; "retrieved content overrode the system prompt at this stage" is actionable.

Risk-rating, reporting, and retest

Rate AI findings by real-world impact and reproducibility, not by how clever the prompt was. A reliable exfiltration of another tenant's data outranks a hard-to-repeat policy slip, and your ratings should say so plainly. Write the report for two audiences at once: an executive summary that states business risk, and technical detail an engineer can act on directly. Every finding should carry a concrete remediation, not "add more guardrails." Close the engagement with a retest that verifies the fixes work and, just as important, that they did not open a regression elsewhere.

Final word

A threat-led AI penetration test is a disciplined engagement, not a bag of jailbreaks. Scope the whole system including its tools and permissions, write rules of engagement that account for side effects, plan around real adversary goals, capture reproducible evidence for everything, rate by impact, and finish with a retest. That is what separates a report a client trusts from a transcript of clever prompts.
